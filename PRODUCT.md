# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Commissioners and "league nerds" — the person in every fantasy league who runs the spreadsheet, posts the recap, and stirs the group chat. They play multiple leagues across ESPN, Yahoo, and Sleeper, and they're as comfortable arguing about category breakdowns as they are about waiver wire moves. They're the connector node in their group: if they like the product, the rest of the league hears about it. Their context is rarely solo — they're usually mid-conversation in iMessage or Discord when they pull something up.

## Product Purpose

Ultimate Fantasy Dashboard pulls a user's leagues (ESPN, Yahoo, Sleeper) across football, baseball, basketball, and hockey into one place that's stronger than any single platform's native tools — power rankings, category breakdowns, matchup analysis, draft tools, share-cards. Success is two-sided: (1) the commissioner connects a league and immediately has things worth screenshotting back into the group chat, and (2) the league nerds in that group chat sign up too. The landing page's job is to get to step 1 fast — get a league connected, free, before any pricing conversation.

## Brand Personality

**Playful, bold, loud.** Three words: confident, group-chat-native, opinionated. The voice has a take and isn't afraid to be funny. Sleeper.com is the closest peer — high color energy, strong typography, illustrated personality, a sense that the people building it actually play fantasy. Not sterile, not sportsbook-aggressive, not corporate. The emotional goal on landing is "oh, finally — this gets it."

## Anti-references

- **Generic SaaS landings.** No gradient hero with floating screenshots, no 3-column "Feature / Feature / Feature" cards with lucide icons, no "Trusted by" logo strip, no pricing table as the page-2 reveal. This is the single biggest trap to avoid — it's where ChatGPT'd landings live.
- **Boomer fantasy-tool aesthetic.** ESPN/Yahoo Fantasy circa 2015 — cluttered tables, beveled buttons, every-stat-on-screen density, zero point of view.
- **Sportsbook / gambling vibes.** No yellow-and-black urgency, no "BET NOW" energy, no neon. Loud is fine; casino-loud is not.
- **Stock fantasy clichés.** No helmet logos, no faux-stadium backgrounds, no clip-art football textures, no ESPN-red-and-blue.

## Design Principles

1. **Group-chat ready.** Every share asset, screenshot, or hero element should look good cropped into iMessage. If a friend sends it to the group, it should make them want to click.
2. **Commish-first.** The hero promise is something a commissioner can immediately bring back to their league. The product makes them look smart; the landing page should signal that.
3. **Loud, but legit.** Bold color and big type, yes — but the user is putting money / pride / standings on the line. Nothing should feel like a casino or a shovelware app. Confidence comes from craft, not gradients.
4. **Free first, paywall later.** Primary CTA is *connect a league* (zero friction). Pricing exists on its own page. The landing page does not lead with "Upgrade".
5. **Cross-platform / cross-sport is the wedge.** ESPN + Yahoo + Sleeper × football/baseball/basketball/hockey is what no native tool does. The landing page should show that off, not bury it.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA as the baseline — readable contrast on bold colors, visible focus states, alt text on imagery, keyboard navigation across CTAs. Don't over-engineer beyond AA on this pass, but don't sacrifice it for visual maximalism either.
